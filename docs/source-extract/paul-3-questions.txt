Question 1: Provide an example of a risk, data or reporting solution you personally delivered
“Certainly. Across my career, I have delivered five significant reporting and data products covering risk and controls, trading operations, lending, finance and commercial performance.
My strongest example is a consolidated risk and control reporting solution that I personally delivered at UBS. I was positioned within technology and transformation, aligned to first-line risk and control, and supported technology, operations, risk and treasury stakeholders.
The business problem was that risk information was spread across spreadsheets, project reports, incident records, control registers and remediation trackers. Senior management did not have one reliable view of control failures, overdue actions, technology dependencies or emerging operational risks.
I personally led the requirements workshops, defined the KPI structure, agreed the authoritative data sources, documented thresholds and escalation rules, reviewed the information model, investigated data inconsistencies, validated the dashboard against source records and coordinated user-acceptance testing.”
Reports and dashboard views delivered at UBS
“The consolidated solution contained several connected reporting views:
Executive risk overview, showing high and critical risks, deteriorating trends and matters requiring a decision.
Control-effectiveness report, showing failed controls, control exceptions and recurring weaknesses.
Incident and operational-risk report, showing significant incidents, repeat incidents and affected systems or processes.
Remediation and action-ageing report, showing overdue actions, accountable owners and target dates.
Data and reconciliation exceptions report, showing failed feeds, unresolved breaks and reporting-quality issues.
Risk acceptance and governance report, showing accepted risks, approval status and upcoming expiration dates.
These were connected views within a consolidated reporting product rather than six disconnected manual reports.”
Risk knowledge demonstrated
“The reports demonstrated working knowledge of operational-risk and governance concepts because they connected risks, controls, incidents, systems, owners, risk acceptances and remediation actions.
They also supported trading and treasury stakeholders because technology incidents, failed interfaces, stale data and unresolved reconciliation breaks could affect the completeness or timeliness of transaction, position and exposure reporting.
I was not calculating market risk or making trading decisions. My role was to make the supporting control and data environment transparent and actionable.”
How you delivered it
“I followed an end-to-end product lifecycle:
First, I clarified the management decisions. I asked what management needed to know, what action should follow an amber or red indicator and which matters required escalation.
Second, I defined the KPIs. For each measure, I documented the definition, purpose, calculation, source, owner, reporting frequency, threshold and drill-down requirement.
Third, I developed the logical information model. We connected risk, control, incident, system, owner, issue and remediation action so that management could move from an executive indicator to the underlying problem.
Fourth, I worked with the technical team on data mappings and transformations. This included standardising risk classifications, system names, ownership and action statuses.
Fifth, I established validation controls. These included record-count reconciliation, duplicate checks, mandatory-field checks, stale-data flags and source-to-report traceability.
Sixth, I reviewed the dashboard design. The executive view was concise, while risk managers and analysts could drill into the relevant system, control, issue or action.
Finally, I coordinated testing, stakeholder sign-off, user guidance and BAU ownership.”
Outcome
“The solution replaced fragmented reporting with one governed view. It reduced manual consolidation, improved the consistency of risk information, identified overdue actions earlier and gave management clearer evidence for escalation and remediation decisions.
That is directly relevant to AGL. The underlying data at AGL might include electricity and gas positions, valuations, counterparties, limits, collateral, liquidity measures and control exceptions, but the delivery discipline is the same.”
