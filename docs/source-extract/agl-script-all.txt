Important treasury risks
Liquidity risk
The risk that the company does not have enough available cash or liquid resources to meet its obligations when they become due.
Funding risk
The risk that funding becomes unavailable, insufficient or too expensive.
Interest-rate risk
The risk that interest-rate movements negatively affect borrowing costs, investment returns or the value of financial instruments.
Foreign-exchange risk

Collateral and margin risk
As market prices move, an organisation may be required to provide additional cash or security against trading positions.

3. What does “energy-market environment” mean?
This means understanding how electricity and gas are generated, bought, sold, dispatched, hedged, settled and monitored.
In the National Electricity Market, electricity supply and demand must be balanced continuously. Generators offer electricity into the market, and the market operator dispatches generation according to demand, bids, network constraints and system requirements.
The NEM operates with five-minute dispatch and five-minute settlement. The move to five-minute settlement aligned physical dispatch and financial settlement and created better price signals for fast-response assets such as batteries and demand response. [aemc.gov.au], [aemo.com.au]
Key energy-market concepts to know
Spot market
Electricity is physically bought and sold at market prices that can change every five minutes.
Hedge or contract market
Companies use financial contracts to reduce uncertainty about future energy prices.
Examples include:
Swaps
Caps
Futures
Options
Power purchase agreements
Physical position
Exposure arising from actual electricity generation, gas supply, customer demand, battery operation or energy delivery.
Financial position
Exposure arising from contracts and derivatives used to manage commercial uncertainty.
Portfolio position
The combined view of:
Customer demand
Generation assets
Gas and fuel arrangements
Renewable output
Battery positions
Purchased energy
Hedging products
Environmental obligations
Dispatch
The process of deciding which generators or assets should produce electricity during each market interval.
Settlement
The financial process that determines what market participants pay or receive for electricity bought and sold.
Market volatility
Energy prices can move sharply because of:
Weather
High or low customer demand
Generator outages
Gas-supply constraints
Low wind or solar production
